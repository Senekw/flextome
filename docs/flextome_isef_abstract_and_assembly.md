# FlexTome, ISEF project abstract (≤250 words) and assembly instructions

## Abstract (Engineering Mechanics / Biomedical Engineering)

Cancer diagnosis depends on 3 to 5 µm paraffin sections, but the rotary microtomes that cut them cost US$9,000 to 22,700, and much of sub-Saharan Africa has one pathologist per million people. The thinnest published open-source sectioning device cuts at 50 µm. I designed FlexTome, a rocking-arm microtome printed on a desktop FDM printer and finished with hardware-store parts for US$61.97. Three decisions make 5 µm sectioning plausible in printed plastic: the arm pivot is a cross-strip flexure of spring-steel feeler-gauge strips, so the knife-to-specimen loop has no sliding joint and no backlash; the 5 µm feed comes from a differential screw (M8 × 1.25 against M8 × 1.0, 0.25 mm/rev) indexed by a 50-tooth ratchet that the arm releases early in each down-stroke, so the micrometre chain is entirely steel; and the disposable blade rides on a monolithic printed flexure stage. I built a parametric CAD model (35 parts), an analytic stiffness and error budget, plane-stress finite-element models of the stage, the feed-screw upstand and the frame, and a Monte-Carlo thickness simulation. The frame FE showed a tower compliance eight times larger than beam theory predicted and drove a gusset redesign; an independent review of the first release then showed that the feed-screw upstand, blade clamp and coupler did not form the load path the budget assumed, and the design was rebuilt around a connected, audited load path. Revision 2 predicts a loop stiffness of 0.83 N/µm and sections of 5.0 ± 0.7 µm (1 s.d.), comparable to the >±10 % scatter measured on commercial instruments, with structural resonances ≥357 Hz against <9 Hz cutting excitation. These are model predictions; a seven-step build-and-measure protocol accompanies the open-source files.

## Assembly instructions

### A. Print (≈27 h total on a 0.4 mm nozzle)
| Part | Material / settings | Orientation |
|---|---|---|
| frame (235 × 150 × 173 mm) | PLA, 0.2 mm layers, 4 perimeters, 100 % infill in tower/gusset and deck, 30 % elsewhere | Upside-down (rib pockets open upward), or split at x = 110 mm for small beds |
| arm | PLA, 0.2 mm, 4 perimeters, 40 % gyroid | Beam horizontal, clamp end up |
| stage_body_flexure | PETG or ASA preferred (PLA acceptable), 0.1 mm layers, 100 % infill | Flexure plates vertical |
| blade_holder, cassette_upper_jaw, conrod, crank_disc (2 webs), handwheel, handle, feed_knob, ratchet_wheel_50T (drum), thickness_selector_shield, pawl_rod, screw_coupler, pivot_clamp_plates ×6 | PLA, 0.2 mm, 100 % infill for the ratchet drum, coupler and blade holder | Flat faces down; blade holder with its back wall vertical |

### B. Hardware to buy (see `flextome_bom.csv`)
Feeler-gauge set (0.20 mm and 1.0 mm blades used), 2 × 608ZZ bearings, 8 mm shaft cut into two 60 mm stubs, 2 × M8 × 250 threaded rod + 4 nuts, M8 × 1.25 rod 55 mm + 1 nut + 1 thin nut, M8 × 1.0 hex bolt 25 mm + 1 fine nut, 2 × M8 washers (16 mm OD), 13 × M3 screws + heat-set inserts, 2 × M4 × 12 cap screws + inserts (blade clamp), 4 × M4 × 12 cap screws + inserts (holder), M5 thumbscrew, M5 shoulder bolt, 3 × M4 set screws, 2 × M6 knob bolts + inserts, M6 × 40 bolt, 2 extension springs 5 × 20 mm (~5 N each), one light compression spring, thread locker, one box of disposable low-profile blades.

### C. Build sequence
1. **Frame prep.** Press heat-set inserts into the deck (2 × M6), the tower pivot bosses (6 × M3) and the arm root bosses (6 × M3); the M3 holes sit beside each strip pocket. Pass the two M8 × 250 tie rods through the base bores and tighten the end nuts hand-tight + ¼ turn (pre-compression against creep; also joins a split base).
2. **Pivot strips.** From the 0.20 mm feeler blade, shear three strips: 10 × 32 mm, 20 × 32 mm, 10 × 32 mm. Deburr edges with 600-grit paper. Lay each in its angled pocket on the tower head (6 mm clamped), fit the printed clamp plate, torque the M3 screws evenly. The 10 mm strips lie in the outer pockets (one orientation), the 20 mm strip in the centre pocket (opposite orientation), forming an X when viewed from the side.
3. **Arm.** Offer the arm root to the free strip ends; clamp with the three remaining plates. Check that the arm swings ±12° freely with no rubbing on the tower or gusset.
4. **Crank drive.** Press the 608 bearings into the pillow blocks. Fit one 8 mm shaft stub through each bearing and lock a crank web on the inner end of each (M4 set screws) so the two webs face each other 12 mm apart under the arm. Fit the M5 shoulder bolt through both webs as the crank pin with the conrod's lower eye between the webs; fit the handwheel hub (M4 set screw) and the handle on the long stub. Connect the conrod fork over the arm pin at 60 mm from the pivot. Turn the handwheel: the arm must rock smoothly through one full cycle.
5. **Blade stage.** Drop a 16 mm washer into each nut pocket, then press the M8 × 1.25 nut into the upstand pocket (it opens toward the coupler) and the M8 × 1.0 nut into the hanging tab pocket (opens toward the front). Press the M8 × 1.25 thin nut into the coupler's rear pocket. Thread the M8 × 1.0 bolt through the tab nut from the front and seat its hex head in the coupler's front pocket. Screw the M8 × 1.25 rod through the frame nut and through the coupler's thin nut until its end bears firmly on the bolt head; lock with thread locker. Slide the ratchet drum onto the rod behind the upstand, lock with jam nuts at both ends; fit the knob. Hook the two extension springs between the tab pins and the upstand pins. Fit the pawl rod in its column (finger through the column slot toward the drum, flag over the striker bar's path), with the light compression spring under the flag. Fit the selector shield's strap to the column with an M3 screw.
6. **Blade.** Bolt the blade holder to the platform (4 × M4). Lay a blade in the 0.20 mm recess on the holder's inclined back wall (edge up, protruding 2.5 mm above the wall top), place the 1.0 mm feeler strip (84 × 14 mm, two holes) over it, and tighten the two M4 cap screws below the blade until the strip lies flat; the blade must not move under finger pressure. **Always handle blades with forceps.**
7. **Mount the stage.** Place the stage on the deck slots, fit the two M6 knob bolts loosely, fit the M6 coarse-approach screw in its side boss against the stage tab.
8. **Alignment.** Clamp a blank paraffin block in the cassette clamp. Back the fine feed to mid-travel (coupler centred in its gap). Wind the coarse screw until the block face is ~0.5 mm from the edge, lock the knob bolts. Set the selector to 6 teeth (30 µm) and trim until the full face cuts; then select 1 tooth (5 µm) and cut a ribbon.

### D. Status
Every performance number in this document is a model prediction. Nothing has been printed, assembled or measured yet; the report's validation plan (loop stiffness with a 2 N load and a dial gauge, feed per tooth over 50 teeth, then 100 consecutive 5 µm sections by profilometry or weighing) is the next step.

### E. Safety
The blade is surgical-grade; fit and remove it with forceps, cover it with its guard when not sectioning, and keep fingers out of the arm's swept volume while the handwheel turns.
